# Case dossier: Tashkent city Kengash "Dizayn-kod" decision (17 Feb 2026)

This is about the **decision**, not people. Bodies are named; individuals appear only when quoting their own public
statements. Every fact has a source and date (listed at the end); **Unknown** = not found in public sources
(checked 18 Nov 2026). Quotes are kept short.

## 0. In one paragraph

A citywide design-code for facades, signs and advertising was drafted for the Tashkent city hokimlik and posted for
public discussion (regulation.gov.uz, Dec 2025 – 10 Jan 2026). The Tashkent city Kengash adopted it on 17.02.2026,
and it took effect about 17.05.2026. Sign removals had already started under older rules, before the code was in force.
By late July, 3,228 businesses on 56 streets were in the rollout. Restaurant chains appealed to parliament, a deputy
called it "breaking the back of business", and the Business Ombudsman found it was adopted **without the mandatory
Ombudsman agreement** and with undefined norms. The hokimlik later admitted the objections were valid. In October 2026
the government changed the national rules (VMQ No. 563: a business's own signboard is not advertising) and opened a new
discussion until 10.12.2026. The Kengash decision itself has not been reported as amended or cancelled.

## 2. What it required, and how it was enforced

**Requirements** (Kengash decision and its annexes):
- **Facades:** materials, colour schemes, textures, shop windows, entrance decoration (Kun.uz 12.03.2026).
- **Signs and advertising:** sizes, fonts, lighting, permitted placement; **"low-saturated"/"neutral" colours** (Spot.uz
  25.04.2026).
- **Streets and fees:** a list of tourist and gastronomic streets with a special regime, plus an **operating fee** for
  landscaping and sanitation of public spaces (amount **unknown**) (Kun.uz 12.03.2026; Spot.uz 07.11.2026).
- **A city commission** reviews design-code projects. District hokimliks must submit public-space boundary schemes
  within 2 months (Kun.uz 12.03.2026).
- **Deadline:** 3 months from publication, with no phased transition (Ombudsman 25.04.2026).
- **Who pays:** the business. No subsidies or compensation are reported (UzA 21.08.2026 mentions none).

**Rules applied alongside it** (national, not the code):
- signs over 1 m² count as advertising and need a paid passport;
- Uzbek must be the main text, with any foreign language at most 40 % of the area;
- the Competition Committee can fine **50 BHM (≈ 20.6 mln so‘m)** (Kun.uz 17.03.2026).

**Enforcement as reported:**
- forced sign removals, sometimes with no written warning, no time to appeal and no named violated norm (Kun.uz 23.04.2026);
- threats to cut electricity (Kun.uz 27.04.2026; 09.06.2026);
- 2-day deadlines (Kun.uz 17.03.2026) and 10-day rooftop orders (Kun.uz 14.07.2026);
- damage claims: "broke letters, tore the canopy, damaged cables" (shop owner, Spot.uz 09.06.2026 related headline).
- Fines actually imposed: **unknown.**

**Numbers:**
- **3,228 businesses on 56 streets** in the rollout (11.05–26.07.2026); 509 had updated facades and 2,627 were finishing
  (Spot.uz / UzA 21.08.2026).
- First removal phase: **100 km of Tashkent's 472 km** busiest roads (Yunusobod, Mirzo Ulug‘bek first) (Kun.uz
  23.04.2026).
- One chain (Evos): 80+ outlets, 65 with passports, 80 mln so‘m a quarter, ≈ **20 mln so‘m per sign removal**, with no
  compensation (Kun.uz 27.04.2026).
- Reported losses: "100 mln so‘m in two months" (Dodo Pizza, 28.04.2026) and "$12k" (15.04.2026) (Spot.uz 09.06.2026
  related headlines).
- Single banners: about $400, and another about 10 mln so‘m, removed without notice (Kun.uz 17.03.2026).
- Rooftop passports: 270.4 mln + 69.4 mln so‘m paid by one company (Kun.uz 14.07.2026).
- **Total signs removed and total business cost: unknown.**

## 3. Voices (short, attributed)

- Deputy B. Bekmurodov: the city's look must not be built by "breaking the back" ("belini sindirish") of business; "laws
  have no retroactive force" (Kun.uz 17.03.2026; Anhor 17.03.2026).
- Six restaurant chains: they support improvement, but legal certainty and "procedural guarantees" must be respected
  (Kun.uz 23.04.2026).
- An unnamed entrepreneur: "Let them say what to change, we don't refuse" (Kun.uz 23.04.2026).
- Korzinka: without a sign, "people think the shop has closed" (Spot.uz headline 27.04.2026).
- Businesses: "We pay for air" (Spot.uz headline 27.04.2026).
- Advertising lawyer M. Mahmudov: before entry into force the code could not be a basis for inspections or removal
  demands (Spot.uz 04/09.06.2026, as summarised).
- D. Imomniyozov (TDYU): the line between a signboard and advertising is unclear, which invites subjective enforcement
  (Kun.uz 29.04.2026).
- Business Ombudsman: restrictions and coercion only "where legal grounds exist" (Kun.uz 09.06.2026).
- Tashkent city hokimlik: the objections were valid; the focus is now open dialogue and case-by-case review (UzA
  21.08.2026).

## 4. Legal analysis, norm by norm

| # | Provision or practice | Conflicts with | Who said it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adopted without **Business Ombudsman agreement** | the rule that business-affecting normative acts must be agreed with the Ombudsman ("current law"; **exact article unknown**; the lawyer must cite it) | Ombudsman 25.04.2026 | not reported fixed |
| 2 | Undefined colour criteria ("neutral", "low-saturated") | legal certainty; Mezon maps it to adliya's own expertise checklist ("corruption-enabling provisions", "terms defined": order 3333 arts. 93–95, **our mapping, not an official finding**) | Ombudsman 25.04.2026 | open |
| 3 | Mandatory uniform colours | registered trademarks / IP rights (statute not named) | Ombudsman; the six chains | open |
| 4 | 3-month deadline, no phasing; new costs | the moratorium on new obligations for business (source act **unknown**) | Deputy 17.03.2026; Ombudsman | partly eased (case-by-case, Aug 2026) |
| 5 | Cancelling rooftop passports valid to 2030/2034 | NLA **art. 41** (no retroactive effect); Admin Procedures **art. 12**; passport rights under the Law on Advertising | Ombudsman 16.07.2026 | Adliya: a court matter (Admin Procedures art. 59) |

**"Why did legal expertise allow it?"**
- **No official statement found** on whether, or how, the draft passed adliya legal expertise. **Unknown.**
- The only Adliya position found is about **competence** (the Kengash may adopt it). It doesn't address points 1–5.
- **The precedent:** in 2021 the Ombudsman said a Tashkent city Kengash decision on outdoor advertising (27.03.2021,
  cited No. 323/37-6) **was not passed through adliya expertise at all** (NLA arts. 21–22), while structures were being
  dismantled on its basis (Kun.uz 28.05.2021). It's the same body and topic, five years apart.

## 5. Similar decisions elsewhere

- **Samarkand:** the regional administration plans its own outdoor-advertising design-code (max banner sizes, fonts,
  facades, colours), to go to the regional Kengash, rolled out street by street. No public discussion is mentioned. Not
  adopted as of the report (Kun.uz 25.05.2026). **The same risk pattern is coming to a second city, which is a live Gate 0
  case.**
- Bukhara, Namangan, Andijan: **none found.**
- National: VMQ No. 563 (13.10.2026) now limits what a local design-code can treat as advertising (UzDaily / Zamin, Oct
  2026). A local code that ignores it would be a **check 1/4** flag (conflict with a higher act in force on that date).
